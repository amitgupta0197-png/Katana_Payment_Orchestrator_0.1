1. About this guide

1.1. Objectives and target audience

This guide provides detailed information on how to connect to and use the Katana Payment Gateway by integrating it into a merchant website or application. It covers the steps in the payment process and the information that must be passed from merchant web servers to Katana, to enable Katana to process payments.

It is written for the merchant's technical team. Additional gateway integration options are also described.

Items marked DECISION are commercial or operational values Katana has not yet fixed. They are shown as open rather than filled with a plausible number — ask your account manager for the current value before relying on one.

1.2. Integration directions

Katana onboards a counterparty in one of two directions. Which applies is agreed at onboarding, and a single counterparty may use both.

Sections 2–11 document Direction B, where Katana issues the credentials. Direction A uses the same field vocabulary and hashing rules in reverse: the counterparty supplies the base URL, Pay ID / MID, client ID, secret, signing scheme with a worked example, endpoint paths and their callback specification; Katana signs outbound calls with their secret and receives their notifications on a callback URL issued at onboarding.

2. Introduction

The Katana Payment Gateway is a secured payment orchestrator. You redirect customers from your website, e-commerce or m-commerce platform to make a payment using UPI, or you take the UPI Intent deeplink and present it inside your own payment page. Katana collects the payment in a secured manner and processes the transaction.

After the payment is complete, the customer is returned to your website and you receive a real-time notification of the payment, including the details of the transaction.

2.1. Pre-Requisite

  • Read this guide in full before starting. The integration is straightforward for anyone with a technical background.
  • A Katana merchant account, with a Key and Salt issued to it (§6.2).
  • Confirmation of which signing scheme your Key was issued with (§6.1). Signing with the wrong one fails every request.
  • A server able to POST JSON and compute an HMAC/SHA-256 or SHA-512 hash. No SDK is required and any language works.
  • A collection UPI ID configured on your Katana account, so payments reach you and not a default (§5.1).
  • An https callback endpoint that is publicly resolvable (§8).

2.2. Connecting to the Payment Gateway

Connecting to the Katana Payment Gateway requires adding Katana as a payment method on your checkout or payment page. When your customer selects it, your server creates a signed order with Katana and receives back a payment URL and a set of UPI deeplinks. You then either redirect the customer to the Katana hosted payment page, or present the deeplink or QR inside your own page.

Order creation is server-to-server. The endpoint returns a JSON body, not a browser redirect — a form posted directly from the customer's browser would land them on raw JSON. Create the order from your server, then send the customer to the pay_url you get back.

The Salt is used only on your server, to compute the hash you send and to verify the HASH on callbacks. It must never appear in browser code, a mobile application binary, or a repository.

A simplified explanation of the transaction flow:

  1. The customer is ready to pay on your website and selects the Katana payment option.
  2. Your server calls the Katana order API with the transaction details and a hash, and receives a pay_url, a UPI Intent deeplink and a QR payload.
  3. The customer either lands on the Katana payment page, or taps the deeplink in your own page and is taken into their UPI application.
  4. The customer authorises the payment in their UPI app.
  5. Katana receives confirmation of the credit and marks the order.
  6. Katana POSTs a signed callback to your server, and the customer is redirected back to your return URL.

3. Inceptive fraud prevention

No one knows your business and your customers better than you do, and the same applies to your fraud screening.

Anti-fraud measures combine several tests to work out the likelihood of a transaction being fraudulent:

  • IP geographical location
  • Buying pattern
  • Purchase history
  • Black / whitelists
  • SQL injection checks
  • Proper integration review
  • Manual review

Katana offers all of these tests, but some will have more relevance to your business than others. The way you would manually score the likelihood of fraud may be more than the cumulative effect of a few tests added together.

Controls Katana enforces on every transaction

ControlEffect
Signature verificationAn order whose hash does not verify is rejected 401 and never created.
Blocked-account checkA suspended merchant is refused 403.
Amount validationA missing, zero or negative amount is refused 400.
Final-status lockAn order that reached SUCCESS or FAILED cannot be changed by a later message; a conflicting update is rejected 409.
Duplicate-UTR blockingA bank reference settles exactly one pay-in.
High-value holdLarge amounts are held for operator review rather than resolved automatically (§4.4).
Callback egress guardKatana refuses to POST a callback to a private, loopback or link-local address.

Each control above is enforced server-side on every request; none can be disabled per merchant.

3.1. Fraud analysts

Katana fraud analysts work with you to define the most appropriate screening techniques for your business. This may be a combination of standard tests, custom tests specific to your business, and can also include additional data integration, which could take the form of historical fraud or suspicious transaction data.

4. Payment process

4.1. Redirecting customers to the Katana Gateway (step 1)

When a customer is on the online checkout or payment page on your website, they should be presented with a Pay button.

How to redirect the customer

  • When the customer selects the Katana option, your server posts the transaction details in Table 2 to the Katana order endpoint.
  • Katana returns a pay_url. Send the customer's browser to it.
  • Obtain a secure session identifier on your side before redirecting, so you can tie the returning customer back to the pending order.

Tips for improving the customer experience

  • Any customer parameters you pass — name, email, phone — pre-populate the relevant fields on the Katana payment page, making it easier for the customer to complete.
  • Redirect the customer in the same browser to maximise conversion.
  • On a mobile device, prefer the UPI Intent deeplink (§4.2) — it removes a page load and drops the customer straight into their UPI application.

4.2. UPI Intent deeplink flow

Where you would rather keep the customer inside your own page, use the deeplink and QR payload returned with the order instead of redirecting.

Field in the responseUse
upi_intentA upi://pay?… deeplink. Bind it to your Pay button on mobile.
deeplinksPer-application variants, for showing named app buttons.
qr_payloadRender as a QR code for desktop checkout or a counter display.
pay_urlThe Katana hosted page, if you prefer not to build a payment screen at all.
The customer usually does not come back. On UPI the customer approves inside their banking application and frequently never returns to the browser, so the redirect alone must never be treated as confirmation. Rely on the callback (§8) and the status enquiry (§7).

4.3. Order lifetime

  
Validity window15 minutes from order creation
At the end of itAn order still PENDING becomes EXPIRED (003) and the callback fires
ExceptionOrders held for review (§4.4) do not expire — they wait for a Katana operator
Late paymentA credit that lands after expiry still counts — see §7.3
Do not display a pay_url, deeplink or QR for longer than the validity window, and never email one — it will usually have expired before it is opened. If the customer has not paid, create a new order with a new txnid rather than re-showing the old one.

Expiry is evaluated when the order is next read or swept, not by a precise timer, so an order may report PENDING slightly past the 15-minute mark.

4.4. Orders held for review

Some orders are parked for a Katana operator instead of resolving automatically:

TriggerEffect on your order
Amount at or above ₹50,000Stays PENDING. Does not expire at 15 minutes. Resolves only when an operator confirms or rejects it.
The payer uploaded a payment proof on the Katana pageSame. The status enquiry returns proof_submitted: true.

When the operator decides, the normal terminal callback fires.

A held order has no documented upper bound on how long it stays PENDING. Your reconciliation sweep must be able to carry an order across days, and your checkout must not assume every order reaches a terminal state inside the validity window. If you cancel a customer's order after 30 minutes of PENDING, you will cancel legitimate high-value payments that are merely awaiting review.

DECISION The ₹50,000 threshold is deployment-wide, not per merchant, and there is no published review SLA. Confirm both with your account manager before designing a timeout.

4.5. The browser return

When an order reaches a terminal state on the hosted page, the customer's browser is redirected to your return_url with three query parameters appended:

https://yoursite.com/return?order_id=ESN78452&status=SUCCESS&rrn=123456789101
These parameters are unsigned and attacker-controlled. Treat them as display only. Anyone can type that URL into a browser. Use them to decide which "thank you" screen to render, and nothing else. Never create, complete, or fulfil an order on the strength of the browser return — confirm server-side with the callback (§8) or the status enquiry (§7) first.

5.2. Amount format

The amount of the transaction is expressed in rupees only. The amount must not contain thousands separators or currency symbols. The value cannot be negative or zero.

For example, INR 12.50 is expressed as 12.50; INR 100 is expressed as 100.

Format the amount once and reuse the exact string. The value you sign must be byte-identical to the value you send. Build the string first, hash that string, then send that same string. A JSON library that re-serialises "100.00" as 100 after you signed it will produce 401 signature mismatch on every request.

The same four inputs signed with two different amount strings produce two completely different hashes:

Key mk_test · Salt s3cr3t · txnid UK108-ESN78452 · productinfo "Order 1001" · email david@example.com

amount = "100"      -> message  UK108-ESN78452|100|Order 1001|david@example.com
amount = "100.00"   -> message  UK108-ESN78452|100.00|Order 1001|david@example.com
                                                  ^^^ different string, different hash
The callback returns the amount in a different format. Katana normalises it: ₹100.50 comes back as "100.5" and ₹100.00 as "100". Compare amounts numerically, never as strings — see §8.1.

5.3. Validating the response

Katana recommends that you validate the transaction details in the response.

If the integration approach is "hosted payment page"

  1. Create a pending transaction or order for a fixed amount on your website.
  2. Redirect the customer to the Katana payment page, where they complete the transaction.
  3. Katana posts the transaction confirmation to your callback URL, including the amount.
  4. Validate the parameters received by recalculating the SHA-256 signature (§6.1 B). If it matches, compare AMOUNT numerically against your pending order (§8.1), and compare ORDER_ID.
  5. Before delivering, require STATUS = "Captured" and RESPONSE_CODE = "000".
  6. Perform a double verification with the status enquiry (§7) — note it uses a different status vocabulary; deliver on status = "SUCCESS".
  7. Compare status values case-insensitively.
  8. Once validated, process the transaction — for example, by dispatching the goods ordered.

If the integration approach is "UPI Intent deeplinks"

  1. Create a pending transaction or order for a fixed amount on your website using the order API.
  2. Take upi_intent (or qr_payload) from the response and present it in your own payment page.
  3. Katana posts the transaction confirmation to your callback URL.
  4. Validate the signature, amount and order reference exactly as above, then double-verify with §7, then deliver.
Please note: the implementations above are mandatory for all merchants. In the case of any discrepancy at the merchant's end, the merchant will be liable for any resulting losses. Katana will review the merchant system before go-live to confirm this — §10.1 lists what the review covers.

6. Generating a secure hash

Your code creates a secure hash over the transaction request data. Katana creates another secure hash and sends it back to you in every callback, so both directions are verifiable.

6.1. SHA256 signature

Which scheme do I use? Your account is issued with one of the two below, and it is shown next to your Key in your portal. Signing with the wrong one produces 401 signature mismatch on every request. Accounts opened for you by a Katana operator are commonly issued Scheme 1; accounts self-provisioned in the portal default to Scheme 2. Confirm before you write any code.

Scheme 1 — SHA-512, pipe-separated

seq  = key|txnid|amount|productinfo|firstname|email|||||||||||salt
         └────────────── 6 values ──────────────┘└ 10 empty ┘
hash = SHA512(seq)                                   // lowercase hex, 128 characters

There are exactly eleven | characters between email and salt — five for the unused user-defined fields and five reserved, each contributing one empty segment.

Key mk_test · Salt s3cr3t

txnid       = UK108-ESN78452
amount      = 100
productinfo = Order 1001
firstname   = David
email       = david@example.com

seq  = mk_test|UK108-ESN78452|100|Order 1001|David|david@example.com|||||||||||s3cr3t
hash = SHA512(seq) -> lowercase hex

Scheme 2 — HMAC-SHA256

message = txnid + "|" + amount + "|" + productinfo + "|" + email
hash    = HMAC_SHA256( key = KEY + SALT , message )      // lowercase hex, 64 characters
Key mk_test · Salt s3cr3t

message  = UK108-ESN78452|100|Order 1001|david@example.com
HMAC key = mk_tests3cr3t
hash     = HMAC_SHA256(HMAC key, message) -> lowercase hex

Under either scheme, a field you omit is signed as an empty string in its position — the separators remain. Note that firstname is signed under Scheme 1 and not under Scheme 2.

Self-check before your first call. Print your signing string with the separators visible and count them. signature mismatch is almost always a wrong scheme, a reformatted amount (§5.2), or a wrong pipe count — very rarely a wrong Salt. See §9.2 for a full debugging order.

B. Verifying a response or callback from Katana

The callback hash is computed over sorted name–value pairs, which is a different construction from the request hash above. To verify:

  1. Take every field received except HASH.
  2. Sort the field names in ascending byte order.
  3. Join them as NAME=value with a tilde ~ between pairs.
  4. Append your Salt directly to the end of that string, with no separator.
  5. Apply SHA-256 and convert the result to UPPERCASE hexadecimal.
  6. Compare with the HASH field, in constant time.

Empty and null values still take part. Include the name with an empty value. Do not drop the field, and do not substitute the text "null" — either produces a different hash. Hash whatever arrives, not a fixed list. Katana may add fields to the callback. Build your verifier to sort and join every field present except HASH, and ignore fields you do not recognise in your business logic. A verifier hard-coded to today's nine fields will start failing the day a tenth is added.

6.2. Secret key

The Salt is the essential element in generating the hash. It is issued with your Key from Integration → Generate Key + Salt in your Katana portal, and is displayed once.

CredentialHandling
KeyIdentifies your account. Sent with every request.
SaltSecret. Server-side only — never in browser code, a mobile application, or a repository. Signs your requests and verifies Katana's callbacks.

One active pair exists per merchant account. Regenerating immediately invalidates the previous pair, so deploy the new Salt before regenerating in production.

The Salt is the only thing protecting order creation. There is no IP allowlist and no second factor on the API. Treat Salt disclosure as a full compromise of your integration: regenerate immediately and reconcile every order created in the interim.

Verifying a callback — Node.js

function verify(body, salt){
  const got = String(body.HASH ?? "");
  const f = { ...body }; delete f.HASH;
  const str = Object.keys(f).sort()
    .map(k => k + "=" + (f[k] == null ? "" : f[k])).join("~") + salt;
  const exp = crypto.createHash("sha256").update(str,"utf8").digest("hex").toUpperCase();
  return exp.length === got.length &&
         crypto.timingSafeEqual(Buffer.from(exp), Buffer.from(got));
}

Verifying a callback — PHP

Verifying a callback — Python

import hashlib, hmac
def verify(body, salt):
    got = body.get("HASH","")
    f = {k:v for k,v in body.items() if k != "HASH"}
    s = "~".join(f"{k}={'' if f[k] is None else f[k]}" for k in sorted(f)) + salt
    exp = hashlib.sha256(s.encode("utf-8")).hexdigest().upper()
    return hmac.compare_digest(exp, got)

7. Status enquiry

Use this API to verify transaction status before service delivery. It is the authoritative record: the callback is a convenience that can be missed (§8.3), the enquiry always reflects the truth.

MethodEnvironmentURL

Where {katana_order_id} is the order.id UUID returned when the order was created — not your txnid.

The enquiry uses a different status vocabulary from the callback. It returns a lowercase status field and no RESPONSE_CODE at all. Match on status and terminal:

7.1. Status Enquiry Request

GET https://katanapay.co/api/pay-status/8d20c0b6-1d0f-4389-bc0b-d1d9d02bfed6

Long-poll variant

Add ?wait=1 to hold the request open until the order reaches a terminal state, up to approximately 25 seconds. This lets a payment page show the result within about half a second of the credit landing, without polling in a tight loop. On timeout the request returns the current, non-terminal payload — it does not error, so do not write a catch that treats a normal timeout as failure.

GET https://katanapay.co/api/pay-status/8d20c0b6-…?wait=1
Never use ?wait=1 in a batch sweep. Each request holds a connection for up to 25 seconds — a 500-order reconciliation run would hold 500 connections for 25 seconds each. Use the plain form for sweeps and the long-poll form only where a person is watching a screen.
The enquiry is unauthenticated and its response is unsigned. The order UUID is the capability. It provides no cryptographic assurance beyond TLS — for that, verify the callback HASH (§6.1 B).

7.3. Expiry and late credits

Expired is not permanent, and you will not get a second callback. If the customer's payment lands after the order expired, Katana honours it and the order becomes SUCCESS — because the customer really did pay. But Katana sends exactly one callback per order (§8.3), and the Expired callback has already been sent. No further notification is issued.

Practical consequences you must build for:

  • Before you cancel an order, void a reservation or release stock on Expired, re-run the status enquiry.
  • Your reconciliation sweep (§10.2) must re-check every order that ended EXPIRED in the last few days, not just the ones still PENDING.
  • Treat SUCCESS and FAILED as permanent; treat EXPIRED as "probably not paid, verify before acting irreversibly".

8. Merchant callbacks

Use the callback to be notified when the payment flow reaches a terminal status. Share your callback URL with the Katana team and it will be configured for you, or pass notify_url per order to override it.

An issued Key + Salt is a prerequisite for receiving any callback. Katana never sends an unsigned callback, so an account with no signing credentials receives nothing at all — the delivery is recorded as skipped on Katana's side. Confirm your credentials are issued before testing your endpoint.

8.2. Merchant-Response

Reply HTTP 200. A body is optional; the following is accepted and recommended:

{ "STATUS": "TRUE" }
Reply within 8 seconds. Acknowledge first, process afterwards: write the callback to a queue or table, return 200 immediately, and do your fulfilment work asynchronously. A handler that charges a card, sends an email or waits on a third party before replying will blow the deadline, be recorded as failed, and be retried — which is how merchants end up double-fulfilling.
Handle callbacks idempotently. Key your handler on ORDER_ID and make a repeat delivery a no-op that still returns 200. Your ORDER_ID dedupe is what protects you from replay — make it durable, not in-memory. RESPONSE_DATE_TIME is the first-attempt time and is not refreshed per delivery, so it cannot be used as a freshness bound.

Retry guidance

Treat 5xx and network timeouts as indeterminate, not failed. Retry with the same txnid: order creation is idempotent on that value, so a retry returns the original order rather than creating a second one. Back off between attempts rather than retrying in a tight loop, give up after a small number of attempts, and fall back to a status enquiry (§7).

Debugging 401 signature mismatch

This is the most common integration failure. Work down the list in order — the cause is nearly always one of the first three.

  1. Wrong scheme. Confirm whether your Key is Scheme 1 (SHA-512) or Scheme 2 (HMAC-SHA256) — §6.1. Signing with the other one fails 100% of the time.
  2. The amount changed between signing and sending. You signed 100 and sent 100.00, or a JSON library re-serialised the string as a number. Build the string once, sign it, send it (§5.2).
  3. Wrong separator count (Scheme 1). Eleven | between email and salt. Print the signing string and count them.
  4. A field in the hash but not the request, or the reverse. Omitted fields sign as empty strings in position; they do not disappear.
  5. Encoding. Hash the UTF-8 bytes. Non-ASCII in productinfo or firstname is the usual culprit.
  6. Stale credentials. Regenerating a Key + Salt invalidates the previous pair immediately (§6.2). Confirm the deployed Salt is the current one.

Still stuck: send Katana your txnid, your signing string with the Salt redacted, and the hash you computed.

Keep signed free-text fields simple. Avoid |, ~ and = in txnid and productinfo — they are the separators used by the two hashing constructions, and are not escaped. A safe txnid pattern is ^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,60}$.

10. Going live and day-2 operations

10.1. Go-live checklist

  • Production Key + Salt issued; Salt stored server-side only — never in browser code, an app binary, or a repository.
  • Signing scheme confirmed (§6.1) and a signed order accepted end to end.
  • txnid scheme is globally unique and prefixed with your merchant code (§5.1).
  • Your response handler checks reused and compares order.amount (§5.1).
  • Amount is built as a string once, signed, and sent unchanged (§5.2).
  • Collection UPI ID confirmed on your account, and verified on a real test payment (§5.1).
  • Default return_url and callback URL saved with Katana, or passed per order.
  • Callback handler verifies HASH before acting (§6.1 B), compares AMOUNT numerically (§8.1), and returns 200 within 8 seconds.
  • Callback handler is idempotent on ORDER_ID, with durable dedupe.
  • Browser-return parameters are treated as display only (§4.5).
  • Reconciliation sweep runs on a schedule and can carry an order for days (held orders never expire, §4.4).
  • EXPIRED orders are re-checked before being written off (§7.3).
  • A test payment completed in each of: success, failure, expiry, and callback-not-received.
  • Escalation contact and account manager recorded on both sides (§11).

Katana's pre-go-live review

Per §5.3, Katana reviews the merchant integration before enabling production. The review covers: signature verification present on the callback handler; amount and order-reference comparison present; idempotent handling; and a working reconciliation sweep. Have these ready.

10.2. Daily reconciliation

Run a sweep at least once a day. It must be able to close orders the callback never closed — given that the callback is sent once and abandoned after ~31 hours of failures (§8.3), and that held orders can stay open for days (§4.4), this is the safety net the rest of the design depends on.

  1. Select your own orders that are not in a final state, plus any that reached a final state in the last 7 days.
  2. For each, GET /api/pay-status/{katana_order_id} — without ?wait=1 (§7.1).
  3. Apply the status table in §7. Re-check every EXPIRED order at least once (§7.3).
  4. Compare amounts numerically and investigate any mismatch before adjusting your books.
  5. Download the day's transactions from Transactions → Export in your Katana portal and match on RRN against your bank statement. The export carries the date, your reference, amount, status, payment id, bank reference and UPI handle.

What to escalate

  • Any order Katana reports SUCCESS that you have no record of.
  • Any order you fulfilled that Katana does not report SUCCESS.
  • Any RRN appearing against two of your orders.
  • Any RRN that your bank statement does not show (§7.2).

Keep order.id, ORDER_ID, RRN and the raw callback body for every transaction — those four are what every Katana investigation asks for.
